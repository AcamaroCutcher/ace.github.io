RTG in CADES Fellowship

Portland State University, Department of Mathematics & Statistics

Summer 2025 – Present

Modified Fourier Neural Operators for Direct Wildfire Spread Prediction: A FourCastNet Adaptation. Uses the FourCastNet architecture for direct modeling of wildfire propagation. Critical fire drivers, including fuel moisture, vegetation density (LANDFIRE) and topography, are integrated into the model's input channels. Trained on historical fire perimeters, the system learns to predict the evolution of the fire front as a discrete state-space variable. The approach builds on FourCastNet's speed, with the goal of generating fire-spread ensembles far faster than traditional physics models.

Python C++ Machine learning HPC

McNair Scholars Program / CCAR REU

Portland State University, Department of Physics

Summer 2024 – Present

Hybrid Data-Driven and Dynamical Forecasting of the South Asian Monsoon Intraseasonal Oscillation. Dynamical models struggle to predict the active and break phases of the South Asian monsoon, which are driven by northward-propagating rainfall anomalies known as MISOs. This research applies Ensemble Oscillation Correction (EnOC; Bach et al., 2021), which uses the near-regularity of climate oscillations to refine physics-based ensemble forecasts with data-driven ones, improving rainfall prediction three to four weeks in advance for agricultural and hydrological planning.

Python / Julia Machine learning Data-driven forecasting Dynamical models HPC

Math Honors Thesis / LSAMP Research Scholar

Portland State University, Department of Mathematics & Statistics

Fall 2023 – Spring 2024

Minimal Group Embeddings. Classified small finite groups and determined their minimal embedding degree. Used the Sylow theorems to analyze the prime-power subgroups for each order and verify group structure and non-simplicity. Building on Cayley's theorem, which guarantees an embedding into S|G|, calculated the minimum n such that G ↪ Sn, often finding degrees much smaller than the group's order. Thesis (PDF).

Abstract algebra Group theory Sylow theorems Group embeddings

Research Assistantship in Theoretical and Experimental Physics

Western Washington University, Department of Physics & Astronomy

Fall 2023 – Spring 2024

Multiscale Theoretical Modeling of Surface Plasmon Polaritons (SPPs): From Fundamentals to Integrated Photonic Applications. Investigated SPP dispersion relations by applying Bloch's theorem to Maxwell's equations. Treating the multilayer as a one-dimensional plasmonic crystal, the model identifies how periodic modulation creates band structures and forbidden gaps, a guide for tailoring the optical response of superlattice metal–insulator–metal devices.

Maxwell's equations Dispersion relations Bloch's theorem Solid-state physics Mathematica

Research Assistantship in Experimental Physics

Western Washington University, Department of Physics & Astronomy

Summer 2020

Excitation of “forbidden” guided-wave plasmon polariton modes via direct reflectance using a low refractive index polymer coupling layer. Experimental investigation of plasmonic modes in metal–insulator–metal thin-film structures. Using a low-index polymer coupling layer and angle-resolved reflectance spectroscopy, the group excited and characterized guided-wave plasmon polariton modes that are normally inaccessible, and showed how the polymer layer modifies the coupling conditions. Published in PLOS ONE (2022).
