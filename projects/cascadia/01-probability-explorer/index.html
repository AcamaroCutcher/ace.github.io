The "Big One" isn't the most likely one

A damaging deep quake under Puget Sound (85%) is far more likely in the next 50 years than a Cascadia M9 (10–15%). These deep quakes have hit in 1949, 1965 and 2001.

Probability, not prediction

The numbers say how likely a quake is over decades, not when it will happen. This model assumes quakes have no memory: one is equally likely on any day. The report notes that this is probably wrong for a single fault, but simple and reasonable when dates are uncertain.

Doubling the window doesn't double the odds

The curve bends toward 100% but never reaches it. Try τ = 26 years: 50 years gives 85%, while 100 years gives 98%, not 170%. For rare events (large τ) the curve is nearly straight, so the odds grow almost in proportion.

Source: Wirth, E., Frankel, A., Sherrod, B., Grant, A., Dunham, A., Stone, I., and Grossman, J., 2025, Earthquake probabilities and hazards in the U.S. Pacific Northwest: U.S. Geological Survey Fact Sheet 2025–3050, ver. 1.1. doi.org/10.3133/fs20253050
