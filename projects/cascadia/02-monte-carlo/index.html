    How quakes are placed on the map
    • Deep quakes reuse the locations and depths of 411 real earthquakes recorded 35–70 km beneath Puget Sound since 1970 (USGS ComCat), shifted by about 3 km.
    • Crustal quakes land on mapped faults from the USGS Quaternary Fault Database, chosen in proportion to fault length: Puget Sound faults (Tacoma, southern Whidbey Island, Devils Mountain, …), the Seattle Fault Zone for Seattle Fault quakes, and Portland-area faults (Portland Hills, Gales Creek, Mount Angel, …).
    • Cascadia M9 quakes rupture the whole subduction zone (shaded when one happens). The circle marks a random epicenter inside the zone. The zone is traced approximately from the report's Figure 1.
    • Magnitudes: the report only gives thresholds (M ≥ 6.5, M ~9), so sizes follow a Gutenberg–Richter distribution with the report's b-value of 0.8, capped at M7.5 (deep), M7.8 (Puget Sound crustal, after the 923 C.E. Seattle Fault quake) and M7.2 (Portland). M9s range from 8.7 to 9.2, like the 1700 quake. The caps are illustrative assumptions. Circle sizes are scaled for readability, not to physical size.

    A Seattle Fault quake is one kind of Puget Sound crustal quake, so it is simulated as a subset of those events (about 265 ÷ 1,000 = 26.5% of them). The simulation uses the report's no-memory model, so the Cascadia M9 matches the 10% figure, not the 15% time-dependent one.

    85% is not a guarantee

    Roughly 15 in every 100 futures get no deep Puget Sound quake at all in 50 years. Likely is not the same as certain.

    "At least one" often means several

    Deep quakes average about 1.9 per 50 years (50 ÷ 26). Many futures get two or three, just as 1949, 1965 and 2001 came in quick succession.

    More futures, steadier answer

    With 100 futures the share wobbles by several points from run to run. With 10,000 it lands within about 1 point of the formula every time. That is why the formula works: it is the average of endless futures.

    Rates from Wirth, E., and others, 2025, U.S. Geological Survey Fact Sheet 2025–3050, ver. 1.1. doi.org/10.3133/fs20253050. Each future is drawn with a seeded random generator, so a seed always reproduces the same futures.
