The orphan tsunami of 1700

Around midnight on January 27–28, 1700, a tsunami flooded villages along Japan's Pacific coast, but nobody there had felt an earthquake. This "orphan" tsunami was later matched to its parent: a great earthquake on the Cascadia Subduction Zone. That match is how scientists know the date of Cascadia's last M9, January 26, 1700 (report p. 3).

    Detective work: narrowing down the date

    Each clue narrows the window. Click them in order, or all at once.

    A date to the hour

    Few prehistoric earthquakes are dated this precisely. Cascadia's last great quake struck on January 26, 1700, around 9 pm local time.

    Big enough to cross an ocean

    Only a rupture along most of the coast could send a tsunami that still flooded Japan. That's why the report puts the 1700 earthquake at M8.7–9.2.

    It will reach far

    The next M9 will send a tsunami to Pacific Northwest beaches within minutes, and across the Pacific within hours. Know your local tsunami evacuation routes (report p. 6).
