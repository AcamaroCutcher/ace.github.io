The problem: starting from a fire that is already burning

Point ignitions are fine for "what if" studies, but a forecast for a real fire starts hours or days after ignition, when all you have is an observed perimeter: a polygon from an infrared flight, a satellite, or crews on the ground. Two things make "just ignite the whole polygon" wrong:

  • The atmosphere has not seen the fire. A real fire has built a plume, an in-draft and its own local winds over hours. Starting cold, the model's first minutes would have the right fire in the wrong air.
  • Igniting a large area at once releases the heat of hours of burning in a few minutes: an unrealistic burst that triggers a spurious convective bubble. (The lab below shows how large that burst is.)

The approach developed for WRF-SFIRE (Mandel et al., 2012, 2014; used for the 2007 Santa Ana fires by Kochanski et al., 2013) is to build an artificial fire history that ends exactly at the observed perimeter, and to replay it in the coupled model: during a spin-up period the fire area is prescribed, but its heat goes into the atmosphere normally, so the air develops the fire's circulation. When the replay ends at the perimeter time, the model takes over and spreads the fire on its own.

Building a fire history from a perimeter

The history is a fire-arrival-time field \(T(\mathbf x)\) on the fire mesh. Given an ignition point \(\mathbf x_{ig}\) at time \(t_{ig}\) and the perimeter \(\Gamma\) observed at \(t_p\), it must satisfy

$$\begin{aligned} T(\mathbf x_{ig}) &= t_{ig} && \text{the history starts at the ignition} \\ T(\mathbf x) &= t_p && \text{on the observed perimeter } \Gamma \\ t_{ig} \le T(\mathbf x) &\le t_p && \text{inside } \Gamma\text{, increasing away from } \mathbf x_{ig} \end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

Between the ignition and the perimeter there is no data, so \(T\) has to be interpolated. The construction used in the lab and in the script of Section 10 walks along rays from the ignition point: a point a fraction of the way from the ignition to the perimeter (along its ray) burned that fraction of the way through the time window,

$$T(\mathbf x) = t_{ig} + (t_p - t_{ig})\,\underbrace{\frac{r(\mathbf x)}{R(\mathbf x)}}_{\text{fraction of the way along the ray}}, \qquad r = \lVert\mathbf x - \mathbf x_{ig}\rVert \tag{2}$$

Eq. (2)R = distance from the ignition to where the ray through x first crosses the perimeter beyond x. The isochrones are scaled copies of the perimeter, so for a star-shaped fire the burned area grows like (t − t_ig)², as for a fire spreading at constant speed.

A first version of this page used the distance to the nearest point of the perimeter instead of the distance along the ray. In an elongated fire that pushes most of the interior close to \(t_p\) (about 45% of the area igniting in the last ten minutes of an hour), so the heat arrives in a late burst, which is the thing the spin-up is meant to avoid. The ray version spreads it out.

The papers construct the history in the same spirit. Better choices weight the interpolation by the expected spread rate, so that \(S\,\lVert\nabla T\rVert \approx 1\) as the level-set solution would give (faster up-slope and down-wind), and later work estimates \(T\) directly from satellite active-fire detections (Farguell et al., 2021). Any of these gives a field that can be replayed the same way.

Outside the perimeter the history continues past \(t_p\). This script extends it as \(T = t_p + d/S_{ext}\) with a nominal speed \(S_{ext}\) (0.3 m/s). That keeps the level-set function below smooth and distance-like across \(\Gamma\) at the hand-over; the model never uses those times, because the replay stops at \(t_p\).

Replaying it in the model

During the replay, SFIRE does not solve the level-set equation. It sets the level-set function directly from the history, every time step:

$$\psi(\mathbf x, t) = T(\mathbf x) - t, \qquad t \le t_p \tag{3}$$

Eq. (3)module_fr_sfire_model.F: “replay, setting the level set function”: lfn(i,j) = tign(i,j) − time_start, and the fuel fraction is recomputed from tign.

So the burning region \(\{\psi \le 0\} = \{T \le t\}\) grows along the history, the fuel burns out behind it with the usual \(F = e^{-(t-T)/T_f}\), and the heat fluxes \(\phi_h, \phi_q\) of Section 06 heat the air. At \(t = t_p\) the state is exactly the observed perimeter, \(\psi(\cdot, t_p) = T - t_p\), negative inside and positive outside; from then on the level-set equation runs with the model's winds.
