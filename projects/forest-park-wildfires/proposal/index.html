1Project description for the potential partner

Since Euro-American colonization of the western United States, wildfires have become less frequent. Yet decreased fire frequency and climate change have resulted in larger and more destructive wildfires. Without regular fire to limit the accumulation of vegetation, the amount of organic burnable material (fuel) increases. Under warmer and drier conditions (i.e., climate change), fuels become increasingly flammable, increasing the risk of highly destructive (severe) wildfires. In short, while fires have become less frequent due to fire suppression, they have become more destructive due to fuel accumulation and climate change (Haugo et al., 2019). Communities at the interface between forests and urban areas are therefore the most vulnerable to severe wildfires (Barr & Kelly, 2026).

In the Willamette Valley, Euro-American forest management and fire suppression policies have resulted in increased vegetation density, increasing the risk of severe wildfire (Walsh et al., 2010). Forest Park has recently been flagged as at risk of wildfire, posing significant risks to residents and critical infrastructure (Wildfire Risk Reduction in Forest Park, 2026). Yet a holistic assessment of fire risk (i.e., vegetation flammability, fire spread, mitigation options, and resident concerns) is lacking. Furthermore, the potential impacts of fire on critical but vulnerable infrastructure, such as the CEI Hub, as well as the threat of wildfire risk due to CEI Hub impacts, have not been explored.

To address these knowledge gaps, we propose to study Forest Park wildfire risk, community concerns, and cascading hazards. To achieve this goal, we will combine methods from the biological, computational modeling, and social sciences to build a nuanced model of fire risk and inform future mitigation strategies.

CEI Hub: Portland’s Critical Energy Infrastructure Hub, the fuel terminals and energy facilities along the Willamette River below the park, between Linnton and NW Portland.

2The team

Alex Bentley Forest ecology

I’m a forest ecologist studying how plants and forests interact with drought. For this project, I am interested in looking at how plant physiological processes related to water transport and photosynthesis (i.e., how plants “use” water) influence their flammability.

Acamaro Cutcher Computational modeling

I am a computational scientist working on wildfire spread models. For this project, I am interested in learning how coupled wildfire models capture fire spread in Forest Park and around its surrounding communities.

Andrea Baeza Social science

I am a social scientist working on the people side of climate change mitigation and adaptation strategies. I am interested in how people relate to different wildfire mitigation strategies, and in centering resident desires and labor justice when implementing mitigation strategies. For this project I am interested in gauging Linnton / Forest Park / Skyline area residents’ concerns about wildfire risk in connection to the CEI Hub, as well as attitudes towards different mitigation strategies. The objective would be to support resident desires in the implementation of future mitigation strategies as the city addresses Forest Park fire risk.
