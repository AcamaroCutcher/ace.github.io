Terrain for WRF-SFIRE

WRF-SFIRE needs the shape of the ground twice: once for the atmosphere, where hills steer the wind, and once for the fire, which runs faster uphill. Out of the box, the model's preprocessor uses global terrain at about 1 km, which can't see the park's ravines. I replaced it with USGS 3DEP lidar elevation for both, so the wind and the fire stand on the same ground.

Background: Forest Park terrain from USGS 3DEP, tinted with this project's data

Figure 8

The ground under the park

Fire runs faster uphill, so the model needs the ground shape as well as the fuel. Forest Park sits on the ridge of the Tualatin Mountains, from 12 m at the river to 339 m at the crest. Slopes inside the park have a median of 19°, and the steepest 10% exceed 29°, mostly in the ravines facing the Willamette.

This 10 m USGS 3DEP elevation becomes the fire mesh's terrain (ZSF). Rivers in 3DEP are flattened at the water surface (about 2.8 m here), so riverbanks don't get fake cliffs. Four bad cells below sea level at the domain edge were filled from their neighbors.

The inner grid is centered on the park, with 2.7 km between the park and its edge, and sits 1.3 km inside the fuel and terrain data. With the original data box, a grid that size would have run off the data's southern edge, past the park's south tip in NW Portland, so I extended the box.

Figure 10

The same ridge, as each grid sees it

A 9 km cross-section through the middle of the park, perpendicular to the ridge. Each grid averages the ground over its cells:

  • The 30 m fire mesh follows the 10 m lidar almost exactly, including the 195 m ravine at 4.1 km.
  • The 150 m inner atmosphere keeps the main ridge and the river-facing slope.
  • At 1,350 m the slope becomes a few large steps; the ravine disappears and the Willamette sits about 80 m up, averaged with the hillside.

That's why the fire needs its own fine terrain, and why the wind near the park comes from the 150 m grid.

What's exported for the model

  • Atmosphere terrain: 3DEP at 1 arc-second (about 30 m) over all of d01, 6,890 × 4,960 cells from sea level to Mount St. Helens (2,531 m). It agrees with the 10 m data to within 0.06 m on average.
  • Fire terrain: 3DEP at 10 m over the fuel-map area.
  • Setup files: a domain configuration and the lookup-table entries that point the model at these files.

Limitations

  • Not yet run. The grids are a proposal. They still need to pass through the model's preprocessor and a test simulation, which may call for terrain smoothing on the 150 m grid.
  • Approximate averaging. The resolution comparison uses simple block averages; the preprocessor's own averaging will differ slightly.
