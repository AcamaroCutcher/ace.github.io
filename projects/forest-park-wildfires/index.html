Forest Park Wildfires

Forest Park covers about 5,100 acres of forest along Portland's West Hills, with homes, industry and the Willamette River along its edges. This project models how a fire that starts in the park could spread into the neighborhoods around it, using the coupled fire–atmosphere model WRF-SFIRE and probabilistic fire-spread maps.

Progress log

  1. v8 6 Nov 2026

    FIRETEC, GPU labs and all of Portland in 3-D. A new Section 15 on the math of Los Alamos' FIRETEC model and its open Fortran code; the WRF-SFIRE labs now run SFIRE's own level-set update on the GPU over real Forest Park terrain; the 3-D simulator shows every building in the domain and the St. Johns Bridge; and an English / Español / 中文 switch.

  2. v7 5 Nov 2026

    WRF-SFIRE handbook. Eight new sections (06–13): the model's equations with interactive level-set and perimeter labs, installing it on a cluster, a complete Forest Park run, perimeter and multiple ignitions, the WRFx web viewer, and converting output to KML/KMZ and 3-D animation. Also fixed the layout on phones.

  3. v6 5 Nov 2026

    Proposal, photos and a new layout. Added our group's partner proposal (Section 01), split the project into one page per section, added photos of Forest Park, and an ignition animation drawn from the spread model that follows light and dark mode.

  4. v5 4 Nov 2026

    First fire-spread maps. Wrote a fire-spread model in Python on the 30 m fuel map and burned 2,000 simulated fires under each wind scenario (Section 05). In an east wind, 37% of fires leave the park and 29% reach buildings, more than twice the northwest-wind numbers.

  5. v4 4 Nov 2026

    Fire weather. Analysed 26 years of hourly weather from four airports around the park (Section 04). Dangerous dry, windy hours come almost only from the east, mostly in September and October, and an east wind pushes fire uphill on 42% of the park's steep slopes. The worst episodes are now the candidate dates for the first WRF-SFIRE runs.

  6. v3 4 Nov 2026

    Terrain for WRF-SFIRE. Proposed three nested model grids around the park and built their terrain from USGS 3DEP instead of the model's default 1 km data (Section 03). The study area grew about 2 km south and 1 km east so the innermost grid has room around the park, so every fuel-map number below was recomputed.

  7. v2 4 Nov 2026

    Roads and terrain. Open road cells are now firebreaks instead of grass corridors (Figure 4), which removed 1,848 ha of false fuel at the time. Added 10 m USGS terrain, slope and the elevation layer WRF-SFIRE needs for its fire grid (Figure 8).

  8. v1 4 Nov 2026

    First combined fuel map. LANDFIRE fuels in the park, stand-in fuels for the city from pavement and tree canopy, building footprints, and the WRF-SFIRE fuel export.

Data: LANDFIRE LF2025 fuel models FBFM40 and FBFM13 (U.S. Forest Service / USGS); National Land Cover Database 2021 land cover, imperviousness, impervious descriptor (roads) and tree canopy (MRLC); USGS 3DEP 1/3 and 1 arc-second elevation; hourly airport observations (ASOS) via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet; City of Portland Open Data building footprints and park boundary. Fuel models: Scott & Burgan (2005), Anderson (1982). Spread model: Rothermel (1972), Albini (1976), Anderson (1983). Maps and analysis by Acamaro Cutcher.
