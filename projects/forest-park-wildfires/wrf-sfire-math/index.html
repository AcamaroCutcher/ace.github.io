The math of WRF-SFIRE

What WRF-SFIRE actually solves: the atmosphere, the wind at the flames, Rothermel's spread rate, the level-set equation for the fire front, and how burning fuel heats the air. Every equation checked against the model's source code.

Two models, two grids, one loop

WRF-SFIRE couples the Weather Research and Forecasting model (WRF, the atmosphere) to a fire-spread model (SFIRE) that runs on a finer fire mesh inside the innermost atmospheric domain. The fire mesh refines every atmospheric cell by a ratio sr_x × sr_y; in the Forest Park setup (Section 03) the 150 m domain d03 with sr = 5 gives a 30 m fire mesh, the same grid as the fuel map in Section 02.

Every atmospheric time step runs the same loop: the wind is brought down to the fire, the fire front moves and burns fuel, and the burning fuel heats and moistens the air. The air then responds (it rises, draws in surface wind toward the fire, changes the wind the fire feels on the next step). That feedback is what a "coupled" model adds over the steady-wind spread model of Section 05.

Where this comes from

The equations on this page were checked against the OpenWFM WRF-SFIRE source (version W4.4-S0.1, master branch, 2026): phys/module_fr_sfire_phys.F (spread rate, heat fluxes, fuel moisture), module_fr_sfire_core.F (level set, ignition), module_fr_sfire_atm.F (wind interpolation, heat insertion) and Registry/registry.fire (namelist options). Line-level details can change between versions.

The atmosphere: what WRF solves

WRF's ARW core solves the fully compressible, non-hydrostatic Euler equations in flux form on a terrain-following vertical coordinate. Written schematically for the dry-air mass per unit area \(\mu_d\) and the coupled variables \(\mathbf V = \mu_d \mathbf v\), \(\Theta = \mu_d\theta\), \(Q_v = \mu_d q_v\) (Skamarock et al., 2019):

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_t \mu_d + \nabla\cdot\mathbf V &= 0 && \text{mass} \\ \partial_t \mathbf V + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf V\otimes\mathbf v) + \mu_d\,\alpha\,\nabla p + \cdots &= \mathbf F_{\mathbf V} && \text{momentum} \\ \partial_t \Theta + \nabla\cdot(\Theta\,\mathbf v) &= F_\Theta + \textcolor{#e34948}{F_\Theta^{\,\text{fire}}} && \text{heat} \\ \partial_t Q_v + \nabla\cdot(Q_v\,\mathbf v) &= F_{Q_v} + \textcolor{#e34948}{F_{Q_v}^{\,\text{fire}}} && \text{water vapour} \end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

Eq. (1)Mass, momentum, heat and moisture. The dots hide the pressure-gradient and buoyancy details; F are physics tendencies (turbulence, radiation, microphysics, surface). The fire enters only through the red terms.

Time stepping is a third-order Runge–Kutta scheme with smaller acoustic sub-steps for the fast sound waves. At the 150 m resolution of d03 the model resolves the large eddies of the boundary layer and the fire's own plume and in-draft, which is why the innermost domain needs to be this fine.

How the fire heats the air

The fire hands the atmosphere two fluxes per atmospheric cell (sensible heat \(\phi_h\) and latent heat \(\phi_q\), in W/m², averaged from the fire mesh). They are not dumped into the lowest cell: the flux decays exponentially with height above ground \(z\) with an extinction depth \(\alpha\) (fire_ext_grnd, default 50 m), and its vertical divergence becomes the heating rate:

$$\begin{aligned} H(z) &= \phi_h\, e^{-z/\alpha} \\[2pt] \left.\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}\right|_{\text{fire}} &= -\frac{1}{\rho\,c_p}\,\frac{\partial H}{\partial z} \;=\; \frac{\phi_h}{\rho\,c_p\,\alpha}\,e^{-z/\alpha} \end{aligned} \tag{2}$$

Eq. (2)module_fr_sfire_atm.F, fire_tendency: hfx = cp⁻¹ exp(−z/α) grnhfx, then rthfrten = −μ ρ⁻¹ ∂hfx/∂z. The moisture tendency is the same with L_v instead of c_p.

A crown-fire flux, when canopy fuel ignites (surface flux above hfgl = 170 kW/m²), is inserted the same way starting at the canopy height fire_crwn_hgt with its own depth fire_ext_crwn.

The wind the fire feels

Fire spread depends on the wind near the flames, far below the lowest atmospheric level (often 10–30 m up). SFIRE interpolates the horizontal wind to fire_wind_height (6.096 m, i.e. 20 ft, the height Rothermel's wind is defined at) with a logarithmic profile and the roughness length \(z_0\):

$$\begin{aligned} U(z_f) &= U(z_1)\,\frac{\ln(z_f/z_0)}{\ln(z_1/z_0)} && z_f < z_1 \\[2pt] U(z_f) &= U_{k-1} + \left(U_k - U_{k-1}\right)\frac{\ln z_f - \ln z_{k-1}}{\ln z_k - \ln z_{k-1}} && z_{k-1} \le z_f < z_k \end{aligned} \tag{3}$$

Eq. (3)module_fr_sfire_atm.F: below the first model level the log law; between levels, linear in ln z. The result is then interpolated from the atmospheric to the fire mesh.

The roughness and wind height can come from the fuel categories or the atmosphere (fire_wind_log_interp), and an optional wind reduction factor per fuel (windrf, fire_use_windrf) can turn the 6.1 m wind into a mid-flame wind. Under Forest Park's closed canopy this matters a lot: the 20 ft wind above a forest is much stronger than the wind at the litter surface, which is why the prototype in Section 05 used sheltered wind factors.

Rate of spread: Rothermel in SFIRE

For each point on the fire front SFIRE computes a spread rate in the direction \(\mathbf n\) normal to the front. With the default fire_advection = 1 it uses the full wind speed and slope and projects them on the normal:

$$\begin{aligned} S(\mathbf x,\mathbf n) &= \underbrace{R_0}_{\text{no wind, no slope}} + \underbrace{\phi_W\!\left(\lVert\mathbf U\rVert\right)\max(0,\cos\theta_U)}_{\text{wind}} + \underbrace{\phi_S\!\left(\lVert\nabla z\rVert\right)\max(0,\cos\theta_z)}_{\text{slope}} \\[6pt] \cos\theta_U &= \frac{\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf n}{\lVert\mathbf U\rVert}, \qquad \cos\theta_z = \frac{\nabla z\cdot\mathbf n}{\lVert\nabla z\rVert} \end{aligned} \tag{4}$$

Eq. (4)fire_ros + fire_ros_cawfe, module_fr_sfire_phys.F. With fire_advection = 0 the wind and slope are first projected on n (U·n, ∇z·n) and then put in the formulas.

$$\begin{aligned} \phi_W &= R_0\,C\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta_{op}}\right)^{-E} u^{B}, & u &= 196.85\,\min\!\left(\lVert\mathbf U\rVert,\,30\right)\ \text{ft/min} \\[2pt] \phi_S &= 5.275\,R_0\,\beta^{-0.3}\tan^2\varphi, & \tan\varphi &= \min\!\left(\lVert\nabla z\rVert,\,5\right) \end{aligned} \tag{5}$$

Eq. (5)u is the wind in ft/min (Rothermel's units); R₀ is the no-wind, no-slope rate. The total is capped at 6 m/s.

\(R_0\)
no-wind, no-slope (backing) spread rate of the fuel: reaction intensity × propagating flux ratio ÷ heat sink
\(C, B, E\)
wind coefficients from the fuel's surface-area-to-volume ratio σ
\(\beta/\beta_{op}\)
packing ratio relative to its optimum
\(\tan\varphi\)
terrain slope from the fire-mesh topography ZSF

The level-set method

SFIRE does not track the fire front as a list of points. It keeps a smooth function \(\psi(\mathbf x, t)\) on the fire mesh (the variable LFN) and defines the burning region and the front as

$$\Omega(t) = \{\mathbf x : \psi(\mathbf x,t) \le 0\}, \qquad \Gamma(t) = \{\mathbf x : \psi(\mathbf x,t) = 0\} \tag{6}$$

A point \(\mathbf x(t)\) riding on the front keeps \(\psi = 0\), so \(\tfrac{d}{dt}\psi(\mathbf x(t),t) = \psi_t + \nabla\psi\cdot\dot{\mathbf x} = 0\). The front moves outward along the normal \(\mathbf n = \nabla\psi/\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert\) at the spread rate, \(\dot{\mathbf x}\cdot\mathbf n = S\), which gives the level-set equation:

$$\underbrace{\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}}_{\text{change of }\psi} \;+\; \underbrace{S(\mathbf x,\mathbf n)}_{\text{spread rate}}\;\underbrace{\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert}_{\text{steepness of }\psi} \;=\; 0 \tag{7}$$

Eq. (7)Osher & Sethian (1988). Only the normal speed matters; the tangential motion of front points is irrelevant.

The payoff is topology for free: separate fires that meet simply merge (their \(\psi\le 0\) regions overlap), islands of unburned fuel can form, and nothing special has to happen at corners. The fire arrival time \(T(\mathbf x)\), stored as TIGN_G, is the time when \(\psi(\mathbf x,\cdot)\) crosses zero. If the spread rate did not change in time, \(T\) would satisfy the eikonal equation \(S\,\lVert\nabla T\rVert = 1\), the same quantity the minimum-travel-time prototype of Section 05 computed with Dijkstra's algorithm.

Try it: the level-set lab

The lab runs on your graphics card over a real 3.0 × 2.0 km piece of Forest Park at 10 m: USGS 3DEP terrain and this project's fuel map, from the park's south-east slopes down to the NW Portland streets. Its update is a line-by-line port of SFIRE's tend_ls and prop_ls: the five fire_upwinding schemes (ENO is the SFIRE default), the artificial viscosity term fire_viscosity, both fire_advection forms of the spread rate, Heun steps and "extend but never down" at the edges. Click to ignite; switch the view to \(\psi\) itself, the arrival time, or \(\psi\) as a 3-D surface whose intersection with the plane \(\psi = 0\) is the fire front. Try fire_upwinding = 0 to see why upwinding matters.

Why the fire comes out long and narrow

With the default formula the wind term is \(\phi_W\max(0,\cos\theta)\), and \(\cos\theta\,\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert = \mathbf w\cdot\nabla\psi\). The level-set equation becomes \(\psi_t + R_0\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert + \phi_W \max(0,\mathbf w\cdot\nabla\psi) = 0\): the downwind-facing part of the front is simply advected along the wind at the head-fire rate, while the flanks only creep at the backing rate \(R_0\). In a steady wind a point fire therefore grows into a strip about as wide as its ignition. The elliptical shapes of Section 05 came from Anderson's empirical length-to-breadth ratio, a different model. In the coupled model the fire's own in-draft adds cross-wind components and widens the fire; in the lab, try a hill, a lighter wind, or fire_advection = 0.

How SFIRE solves it numerically

Upwinding. \(\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert\) must be taken from the side the front comes from, or the scheme oscillates and blows up (the "No upwinding" option in the lab). With one-sided differences \(D^{\pm}_x\psi\), the Godunov/Sethian form used for \(S \ge 0\) is

$$\begin{aligned} \lVert\nabla\psi\rVert_{i,j} &\approx \sqrt{\max(D^-_x\psi,0)^2 + \min(D^+_x\psi,0)^2 + \max(D^-_y\psi,0)^2 + \min(D^+_y\psi,0)^2} \\[2pt] D^{\pm}_x\psi_{i,j} &= \pm\,\frac{\psi_{i\pm1,j} - \psi_{i,j}}{\Delta x}, \qquad D^{\pm}_y \text{ likewise} \end{aligned} \tag{8}$$

Eq. (8)fire_upwinding = 4 in module_fr_sfire_core.F (prop_ls / tend_ls). Options 1–3 are other upwind selectors; 3 (ENO) is the SFIRE default.

Time stepping. Heun's method (2nd-order Runge–Kutta) with fire_back_weight = 0.5:

$$\begin{aligned} \psi^{*} &= \psi^{n} - \Delta t\,F(\psi^{n}) && \text{predictor} \\ \psi^{n+1} &= \psi^{n} - \Delta t\,\big[(1-w)\,F(\psi^{n}) + w\,F(\psi^{*})\big] && \text{corrector},\ w = 0.5 \\ F(\psi) &= S\,\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert \end{aligned} \tag{9}$$

The fire time step is the atmospheric one (fire_time_step_ratio = 1 by default), and it must satisfy a CFL condition, roughly \(S_{\max}\,\Delta t < \Delta x_f\). At \(\Delta x_f = 30\) m and the 6 m/s cap, any atmospheric step under 5 s is safe; on d03 it is below 1 s anyway.

Reinitialization. As the front moves, \(\psi\) stops being a distance function (its slope near the front drifts away from 1), which degrades the normals and the accuracy. The NCAR code (Muñoz-Esparza et al., 2018) fixes this by solving, in pseudo-time \(\tau\),

$$\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau} + \operatorname{sgn}(\psi_0)\left(\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert - 1\right) = 0 \tag{10}$$

Eq. (10)Moves ψ toward a signed distance without moving the zero level. Toggle it in the lab and watch the “|∇ψ| − 1 near the front” readout.

Burning fuel and releasing heat

Once the fire arrives at time \(T\), the fuel in a cell does not vanish at once: the fraction left decays exponentially with a burn time \(T_f\) set by the fuel's weight parameter (\(T_f = \text{weight}/0.85\)):

$$F(\mathbf x,t) = \exp\!\left(-\,\frac{t - T(\mathbf x)}{T_f}\right) \quad \big(t \ge T(\mathbf x)\big), \qquad T_f = \frac{\text{weight}}{0.85}\ \text{s} \tag{11}$$

Eq. (11)module_fr_sfire_phys.F (fuel_time = weight/0.85) and fuel_left in module_fr_sfire_core.F, which integrates F over 2 × 2 sub-cells of every fire cell (fire_fuel_left_irl/jrl) because the front cuts through cells.

The mass burned in a time step, \(\Delta m = w_0\,\Delta F\) (kg/m², with the initial load \(w_0\) = fgi), sets the fluxes. With the fuel moisture \(M\) (fuelmc_g, default 0.08) and the water fraction \(b = M/(1+M)\):

$$\begin{aligned} \phi_h &= \frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}\,(1-b)\,h && \text{sensible heat} \\ \phi_q &= \frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}\,\big(b + 0.56\,(1-b)\big)\,L_v && \text{latent heat}, \qquad b = \frac{M}{1+M} \end{aligned} \tag{12}$$

Eq. (12)heat_fluxes, module_fr_sfire_phys.F. h = cmbcnst = 17.433 MJ/kg of dry fuel; 0.56 kg of water is released per kg of dry fuel burned; L_v ≈ 2.5 MJ/kg.

Fuel moisture model

With fmoist_run = .true. SFIRE runs a time-lag model for dead fuel classes (by default three: 1-h, 10-h and 100-h fuels, up to six) on the atmospheric grid, driven by the model's own temperature, humidity and rain (Mandel et al., 2014; Vejmelka et al., 2016). Each class relaxes toward an equilibrium moisture with its own time constant:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{dm_k}{dt} &= \frac{E(T,H) - m_k}{T_k}, \qquad k = 1\text{ h},\ 10\text{ h},\ 100\text{ h} \\[2pt] E_{\text{drying}} &= 0.942\,H^{0.679} + 4.99\times10^{-5}\,e^{0.1H} + 0.18\,(294.25 - T)\left(1 - e^{-0.115H}\right) \end{aligned} \tag{13}$$

Eq. (13)module_fr_sfire_phys.F (advance_moisture): separate drying and wetting equilibria (wetting: 0.618 H^0.753 + 4.54e-5 e^{0.1H} + …), H = relative humidity in %, T in K; rain adds a wetting branch. The result is interpolated to the fire mesh (fmoist_interp).

The fire then uses a weighted mix of the classes for each fuel model. By default every fuel model uses only the 1-h class (weights fmc_gw01 = 1, the others 0; set them in namelist.fire). Drying lags are 1, 10 and 100 h and wetting lags 14, 140 and 1,400 h. The option needs fire_fmc_read = 0; the code stops with “fmoist_run=T requires fire_fmc_read=0” otherwise.

Spotting, smoke and what is not modelled

  • Smoke. With tracer_opt = 3 the fire emits a passive smoke tracer, fire_smoke, at fire_tracer_smoke grams of smoke per kg of fuel burned, which the atmosphere carries. WRF-Chem coupling gives real chemistry (Kochanski et al., 2016).
  • Firebrands. The code includes a Lagrangian firebrand spotting module (module_firebrand_spotting.F, developed at NCAR by M. Frediani and T. Juliano, options fs_*; off unless fs_firebrand_gen_lim > 0). Check which fire code path your build couples it to.
  • Not modelled: structure-to-structure spread and ember ignition of homes, suppression, and spread across fuel-free cells (roads, rivers) without spotting. The surface fuel map is the only way the city enters, exactly as in this project's fuel map.
  • Resolution. The front is a sub-grid feature: the level set locates it inside cells, but fuel and terrain are only as good as the fire mesh (30 m here). Fire-induced winds need atmospheric cells of order 100 m or finer to be resolved.
How this connects to Section 05

The prototype in Section 05 solved the steady-wind version of the same problem (Rothermel rates, an elliptical spread shape, arrival times by minimum travel time). WRF-SFIRE replaces the fixed wind with the coupled atmosphere and the Dijkstra solve with the level-set PDE, so wind shifts, fire-induced in-drafts and merging fires all feed back on the spread.
