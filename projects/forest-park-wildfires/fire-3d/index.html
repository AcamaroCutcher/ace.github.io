Forest Park in 3-D

The fire below spreads over the real Forest Park: USGS 3DEP terrain at 30 m and this project's wildland–urban fuel map (Section 02, version 3). The ground is colored from the land cover, a tree stands on every forested cell at its LANDFIRE canopy height, and the sun, sky and shadows follow the simulated clock, starting at 2 pm. Your graphics card solves the level-set equation of Section 06 on all 428,160 cells of the fire mesh, with SFIRE's spread formula and the Anderson-13 coefficients for the chosen weather. Pick a scenario, change the wind, drag to fly around, and click anywhere on the terrain to start a new fire. Surface switches between the natural view and the fuel-map colors; Light holds the scene at midday, sunset or night.

Ember spotting (optional) adds a simple stochastic model: burning areas throw firebrands downwind with a lognormal distance (median about 60 m at 4 m/s wind, growing with wind speed) and ±12° scatter, and a brand that lands on unburned fuel starts a new fire after its flight time. This is not part of SFIRE's ifire = 1 code (the NCAR path has a Lagrangian firebrand module, Section 06); it is here to show how spotting lets fire jump roads and fuel-free gaps that stop a surface fire.

Longer runs. Fires now run for 24 hours. The 24-hour time-lapse button replays a full day at 30 simulated minutes per second while the camera slowly circles the park.

Every building, and the St. Johns Bridge

All 134,000 buildings inside the 19 × 20 km domain stand on the terrain at their real heights, from Downtown, the Pearl and NW Portland to Linnton, Forest Heights, Cedar Mill and St. Johns (City of Portland Building Footprints with lidar-derived heights). The 7,811 buildings of Downtown, Old Town, the Pearl, Goose Hollow and NW Portland are drawn as their true footprints (Wells Fargo Center comes out at 163 m against its real 166 m); the other 126,600 are drawn as boxes that keep each footprint's orientation and area, which keeps all of them in a 1.5 MB download. The same simulator, with the same buildings, runs in Sections 08, 09 and 11.

The St. Johns Bridge crosses the Willamette next to the park's north end: its deck follows the OpenStreetMap line, with the 368 m main span hung from two Gothic towers 124 m above the river and the deck about 62 m above the water.

A building glows when fire is burning in its 30 m cell or the cells next to it and darkens once the ground around it has burned. Buildings are drawn for context: as in WRF-SFIRE, they are fuel-free cells and do not ignite one another. Use Fly to… to visit Downtown, NW Portland, Linnton, St. Johns or the bridge.

The crown-fire add-on

WRF-SFIRE with ifire = 1 spreads fire through surface fuels only. Forest Park is a 25 m tall conifer and maple forest, so the Crown fire switch adds the standard two-step test used by operational fire-behavior tools, with the canopy data of each cell. It is off by default, and like ember spotting it is not part of SFIRE.

$$I = H\,w\,R, \qquad L = 0.0775\,I^{0.46} \tag{1}$$

Eq. (1)Byram's (1959) fireline intensity \(I\) in kW/m, from the heat of combustion \(H\) = 18,000 kJ/kg, the fuel load \(w\) (kg/m²) and the spread rate \(R\) (m/s), and his flame length \(L\) in metres. The full load of the fuel model is used, so \(I\) is an upper estimate.

$$I \ge I_0 = \left(0.010\,\mathrm{CBH}\,(460 + 25.9\,\mathrm{FMC})\right)^{3/2} \tag{2}$$

Eq. (2)Van Wagner's (1977) condition for fire to climb into the crowns: the surface intensity must exceed \(I_0\), which grows with the canopy base height CBH (m) and the foliar moisture content FMC (%, 100 here).

$$R_c = 3.34\,R_{10}\big(0.4\,U\big) \;\ge\; \frac{3.0}{\mathrm{CBD}}\ \text{m/min} \tag{3}$$

Eq. (3)Rothermel's (1991) crown-fire rate: 3.34 times the surface rate of fuel model 10 at 40% of the open wind. The crown fire is active, and the front moves at \(R_c\), only where \(R_c\) exceeds Van Wagner's limit set by the canopy bulk density CBD (kg/m³).

With the dry east wind at 20 mph, most of the park's timber-understory cells (Anderson model 10) pass the first test and torch, but the crown-fire rate stays just under the limit for an active crown fire on flat ground: about 21 m/min against the 25 m/min that the park's mean bulk density of 0.12 kg/m³ requires. Active runs appear on slopes that face the wind and at higher wind speeds. The readout shows the area of active crown fire.

How far to trust it

The crown-fire test uses the fuel model's full load and a fixed foliar moisture, and LANDFIRE's canopy base height is a 30 m estimate, not a measurement of each stand. Use the switch to see where the canopy could carry fire, not to predict it.

What is being solved

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} + S(\mathbf x,\mathbf n)\,\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert &= 0 \\[4pt] S(\mathbf x,\mathbf n) &= R_0 + \phi_W\!\left(\lVert\mathbf U\rVert\right)\max(0,\cos\theta_U) + \phi_S\!\left(\lVert\nabla z\rVert\right)\max(0,\cos\theta_z) \end{aligned} \tag{4}$$

Eq. (4)The level-set equation with SFIRE's default spread rate (fire_advection = 1), as in Section 06. Upwind differences (fire_upwinding = 4), Heun time steps (fire_back_weight = 0.5) at the CFL limit 0.45 Δx / S_max, ψ only decreasing (fire_grows_only = 1).

  • Grid. 640 × 669 cells of 30 m (19.2 × 20.1 km), the fire mesh of the planned WRF-SFIRE domain d03. The fire state lives in two floating-point textures (ψ and arrival time), updated by two shader passes per time step.
  • Fuel and terrain. Anderson-13 categories from the WUI fuel map; category 14 (roads, buildings, water) does not burn and acts as a wall. Slope from the 30 m DEM. The 3-D surface is drawn at 60 m with 1–4× vertical exaggeration.
  • Weather. The two scenarios of Section 04: the dry east wind (20 mph from 84°, 1-h fuel moisture 4%) and a northwest summer afternoon (9 mph from 317°, 7%). With "mid-flame wind" on, the 6.1 m wind is multiplied by each fuel's windrf, as SFIRE does with fire_use_windrf = 1.
  • Burnout, flames and smoke. Behind the front the colour follows the fuel left, \(e^{-(t-T)/T_f}\) (Eq. 11 of Section 06), slowed to at least 90 s so grass fires stay visible. Flames and smoke are drawn from the burning cells; smoke rises and drifts with the wind for the picture, it is not a dispersion model.
What it is not

This is the fire half of WRF-SFIRE only, with a steady, uniform wind. It has no atmosphere: no fire-induced winds, no plume, no terrain-channelled flow, and, unless you switch on the two add-ons, no spotting and no crown fire. A real WRF-SFIRE run (Section 08) adds all of the atmospheric feedback. Treat the shapes and speeds as a way to see the model's fire physics on Forest Park, not as a forecast.

The camera glides: movements ease in and out, a quick drag keeps spinning for a moment, and the camera never goes below the ground. The buttons at the top right zoom, rotate and tilt: tap for a step, hold to keep moving.

  • Raise the playback speed (simulated minutes per second) to watch hours of spread; lower it to see the front move cell by cell.
  • Turn the vertical exaggeration up to see how fires run up the ravines on the park's east face in the east wind.
  • Try the northwest breeze: most fires barely spread through the closed timber litter of the park, the reason the east-wind days matter.
  • The simulation needs WebGL 2 with floating-point render targets (any recent desktop browser, and most phones). Performance depends on the GPU; on integrated graphics keep the playback at 10–20 min/s.

Data and method

  1. Terrain: USGS 3DEP, resampled to the 30 m fuel grid (Section 03). Fuel: LANDFIRE LF2025 FBFM13 inside the park, NLCD-based stand-in fuels outside it (Section 02).
  2. Spread: Rothermel (1972) with the Anderson (1982) fuel models, in the form of WRF-SFIRE's fire_ros_cawfe; coefficients computed with this project's rothermel.py.
  3. Level-set method: Osher & Sethian (1988); WRF-SFIRE: Mandel, Beezley & Kochanski (2011), Geoscientific Model Development 4, 591–610.
  4. Canopy height, cover, base height and bulk density: LANDFIRE LF2025 CH, CC, CBH and CBD at 30 m. Paved share of each cell: NLCD 2021.
  5. Crown fire: Van Wagner, C. E. (1977), Conditions for the start and spread of crown fire, Canadian Journal of Forest Research 7, 23–34; Rothermel, R. C. (1991), Predicting behavior and size of crown fires in the Northern Rocky Mountains, USDA Forest Service Research Paper INT-438; Scott, J. H. & Reinhardt, E. D. (2001), Assessing crown fire potential by linking models of surface and crown fire behavior, Research Paper RMRS-RP-29.
  6. Flame length and fireline intensity: Byram, G. M. (1959), Combustion of forest fuels, in K. P. Davis (ed.), Forest Fire: Control and Use, McGraw-Hill.
  7. Buildings: City of Portland Building Footprints (portlandmaps.com open data), heights from the AVG_HEIGHT field.
  8. Power lines, trails and the St. Johns Bridge alignment: © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License.
