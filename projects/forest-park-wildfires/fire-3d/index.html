Forest Park in 3-D

The fire below spreads over the real Forest Park: USGS 3DEP terrain at 30 m, draped with this project's wildland–urban fuel map (Section 02). Your graphics card solves the level-set equation of Section 06 on all 428,160 cells of the fire mesh, with SFIRE's spread formula and the Anderson-13 coefficients for the chosen weather. Pick a scenario, change the wind, drag to fly around, and click anywhere on the terrain to start a new fire.

What is being solved

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} + S(\mathbf x,\mathbf n)\,\lVert\nabla\psi\rVert &= 0 \\[4pt] S(\mathbf x,\mathbf n) &= R_0 + \phi_W\!\left(\lVert\mathbf U\rVert\right)\max(0,\cos\theta_U) + \phi_S\!\left(\lVert\nabla z\rVert\right)\max(0,\cos\theta_z) \end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

Eq. (1)The level-set equation with SFIRE's default spread rate (fire_advection = 1), as in Section 06. Upwind differences (fire_upwinding = 4), Heun time steps (fire_back_weight = 0.5) at the CFL limit 0.45 Δx / S_max, ψ only decreasing (fire_grows_only = 1).

  • Grid. 640 × 669 cells of 30 m (19.2 × 20.1 km), the fire mesh of the planned WRF-SFIRE domain d03. The fire state lives in two floating-point textures (ψ and arrival time), updated by two shader passes per time step.
  • Fuel and terrain. Anderson-13 categories from the WUI fuel map; category 14 (roads, buildings, water) does not burn and acts as a wall. Slope from the 30 m DEM. The 3-D surface is drawn at 60 m with 1–4× vertical exaggeration.
  • Weather. The two scenarios of Section 04: the dry east wind (20 mph from 84°, 1-h fuel moisture 4%) and a northwest summer afternoon (9 mph from 317°, 7%). With "mid-flame wind" on, the 6.1 m wind is multiplied by each fuel's windrf, as SFIRE does with fire_use_windrf = 1.
  • Burnout, glow and smoke. Behind the front the colour follows the fuel left, \(e^{-(t-T)/T_f}\) (Eq. 11 of Section 06), slowed to at least 90 s so grass fires stay visible. Glow and smoke are drawn from the burning cells; smoke drifts with the wind for the picture, it is not a dispersion model.
What it is not

This is the fire half of WRF-SFIRE only, with a steady, uniform wind. It has no atmosphere: no fire-induced winds, no plume, no terrain-channelled flow, no spotting and no crown fire. A real WRF-SFIRE run (Section 08) adds all of the atmospheric feedback. Treat the shapes and speeds as a way to see the model's fire physics on Forest Park, not as a forecast.

  • Raise the playback speed (simulated minutes per second) to watch hours of spread; lower it to see the front move cell by cell.
  • Turn the vertical exaggeration up to see how fires run up the ravines on the park's east face in the east wind.
  • Try the northwest breeze: most fires barely spread through the closed timber litter of the park, the reason the east-wind days matter.
  • The simulation needs WebGL 2 with floating-point render targets (any recent desktop browser, and most phones). Performance depends on the GPU; on integrated graphics keep the playback at 10–20 min/s.

Data and method

  1. Terrain: USGS 3DEP, resampled to the 30 m fuel grid (Section 03). Fuel: LANDFIRE LF2025 FBFM13 inside the park, NLCD-based stand-in fuels outside it (Section 02).
  2. Spread: Rothermel (1972) with the Anderson (1982) fuel models, in the form of WRF-SFIRE's fire_ros_cawfe; coefficients computed with this project's rothermel.py.
  3. Level-set method: Osher & Sethian (1988); WRF-SFIRE: Mandel, Beezley & Kochanski (2011), Geoscientific Model Development 4, 591–610.
