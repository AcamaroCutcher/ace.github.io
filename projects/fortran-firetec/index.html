Fortran for FIRETEC

Learn the Fortran behind Los Alamos' HIGRAD/FIRETEC wildfire model: from your first program to loops, arrays, matrix algebra, modules, a small fire model of your own, and parallel computing with OpenMP and MPI, ending with a guided tour of the real FIRETEC source.

Nine lessons

Lessons

Each lesson has complete programs to download and exercises. Lessons 01–07 need only gfortran; 08 adds OpenMP and MPI; 09 builds and reads FIRETEC itself. The physics is explained in The math of FIRETEC.

01

Getting started

Why Fortran still runs the big physics codes, installing a compiler on a laptop or a cluster, and your first program.

Open lesson →
02

Variables, kinds and decisions

Types, precision with kind parameters (FIRETEC's wp), constants, arithmetic, formatted output, if and select case.

Open lesson →
03

Loops

Counted loops, do while (FIRETEC's time loop), exit and cycle, and why the order of nested loops decides the speed.

Open lesson →
04

Arrays

Allocatable arrays, custom bounds and halo cells, sections, whole-array arithmetic, masks and the intrinsic functions.

Open lesson →
05

Matrix algebra

matmul, transpose and dot_product; solving linear systems by Gaussian elimination, with the tridiagonal Thomas algorithm, and with LAPACK.

Open lesson →
06

Functions, subroutines and modules

Splitting a program into procedures, intent, pure and elemental functions, optional arguments, and modules the way FIRETEC uses them.

Open lesson →
07

Building a small fire model

Everything so far in one program: a 1-D strip of grass where hot gas is carried by the wind, fuel burns, and the front moves.

Open lesson →
08

Parallel processing: OpenMP and MPI

Threads on one machine with OpenMP, processes across a cluster with MPI, domain decomposition and halo exchange as in FIRETEC, and job scripts.

Open lesson →
09

Reading and running FIRETEC

A guided tour of the HIGRAD/FIRETEC source: the main loop, the state arrays, a physics routine, the build, the input file and first changes.

About the code The programs were written for this course; the 1-D fire model was checked against a NumPy version of itself. Each lesson's files can be downloaded, and build_all.sh compiles and runs them all. FIRETEC excerpts are from FIRETEC-1.5.1, © 2026 Triad National Security, LLC, under the BSD 3-Clause license.

All programs: arrays.f90, build_all.sh, control.f90, fire1d.f90, hello.f90, job.slurm, kinds.f90, lapack.f90, loop_order.f90, loops.f90, matrix.f90, mpi_heat.f90, mpi_hello.f90, omp_sum.f90, plot_fire1d.py, procedures.f90, thomas.f90.
